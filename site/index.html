Why this exists

Clipping is how people find you, and it is the part most creators cannot afford to do. Hiring an editor costs money you do not have yet. Editing it yourself costs a day you needed for something else. And the tools built to solve exactly that are subscriptions — so the creators who need the help most, the ones still growing, are the ones priced out of it.

That is the trade this removes. Clips Studio runs on your own computer, so there is no subscription, no credits, no per-clip charge and no limit on how much you make. If you have a PC and something worth posting, you can promote your channel without paying anybody — me included.

Paid services like OpusClip do this well, and there is nothing wrong with using one. This is for the creators who would rather not pay a monthly fee to post their own work.

How it works

Every step runs locally. The only thing that touches the network in a normal run is downloading the video you asked for.

  1. Transcription faster-whisper with word-level timing, so captions land on the right syllable and clips start on a real sentence.
  2. Signal analysis Loudness spikes, laughter-shaped bursts, scene cuts, motion and on-screen reactions, measured across the whole video.
  3. Scoring A local language model reads the transcript alongside those signals, so a quiet line during an explosive moment is judged correctly.
  4. Framing Pose detection keeps the subject in frame, and in group footage the camera follows whoever is speaking. Crops are never stretched.
  5. Captions and titles Word-synced captions in your style, plus a title, description and hashtags — all editable before you export.

What you get

Finds what transcripts miss

Laughter, hype and clutch moments often read flat in text. Peaks in the audio and video become clip candidates in their own right.

Speaker-aware framing

In multi-person footage the crop follows whoever is talking, detected from mouth movement. Podcast mode frames each camera shot separately.

A real editor

Trim, cut, mute words, add a hook, music and a watermark — all non-destructive. Or describe the change in plain language and let the AI apply it.

19 languages

Translate, subtitle or dub a clip, with every line shown for review before anything is burned in. The app itself is translated into all 19 too.

Learns each creator

Recurring topics, running jokes and ongoing storylines build up per channel and sharpen the titles. It stays on your machine, and you can read or wipe it.

Long-form too

The same analysis produces horizontal clips, a best-of highlight reel, or the whole stream with the dead air removed.

What it's built for

Worth knowing before you download, so you're not guessing why a clip came out the way it did.

  • People on camera, talking. IRL, just chatting, podcasts, vlogs, interviews. This is what the clip-picking and the framing are tuned for, and it's what gets tested on real streams before anything ships.
  • Gaming with a facecam works — your webcam and the gameplay are detected and stacked automatically, so both stay in frame. It's newer than the rest, so treat it as good rather than polished.
  • Gaming with no facecam is weaker. The framing follows a person, and with nobody on screen there's nothing to follow, so it centres the shot instead. Clips still get cut, captioned and edited — the framing just isn't doing anything clever.
  • Reaction content isn't really its thing. Clips are chosen from what's said; it can't watch the video you're reacting to, so the moment that made the clip is invisible to it. Re-uploading someone else's footage is also a copyright question worth settling yourself.

Nothing here refuses to run — feed it anything and it will produce clips. These are the cases where it's doing less thinking than it looks like, and where you should expect to reach for the editor more often.

Meet Clippy

Clipping a three-hour stream means watching a three-hour stream. Clippy the Cat does that part. He sits through the whole thing, notices where the room got loud and where the story turned, and comes back with the bits worth posting.

  • He watches, you choose. Clippy brings back everything that cleared the bar, not a fixed three — you pick what actually goes out.
  • He remembers your creators. Running jokes and ongoing storylines build up per channel, so the titles get sharper over time.
  • He works on your machine. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is metered, and he does not phone anyone about what he saw.

Why local matters

Nothing is uploaded

Unreleased footage, sponsor reads and personal streams never leave your computer. There is no account and no server holding your video.

No per-clip cost

Cloud clippers meter minutes or clips. Running the models yourself costs electricity, so processing a three-hour stream twice is free.

Yours to change

Nobody can take it away, put it behind a paywall, or change the terms on you. Even how it picks clips is yours to adjust.

Getting started

Download ClipsStudio-Web-Setup.exe and run it. A setup wizard takes it from there — it checks your computer and downloads an AI model sized to your graphics card.

What the installer does and doesn't carry. It brings the app, the whole AI engine, every library it needs, FFmpeg and the detection weights — no Python, no PATH, no terminal. It does not bring Ollama, which runs the language model and manages your GPU itself, or the model, which is gigabytes and depends on your graphics card. The setup wizard checks for both and walks you through them, so you'll want an internet connection the first time you run it.
Clips Studio is in alpha. It works and it's used daily, but you will find rough edges. There's a Feedback button inside the app for reporting them — it needs no account and attaches the details automatically. The installer isn't code-signed yet, so Windows will warn the first time you run it: choose More info → Run anyway.
