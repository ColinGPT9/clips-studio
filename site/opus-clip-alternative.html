Opus Clip vs Clips Kitty

A free Opus Clip alternative that runs on your own PC

Clips Kitty is an open-source alternative to Opus Clip. The difference that matters is where the work happens: your video never leaves your computer, so there is nothing to meter and nothing to charge for.

It is a Windows desktop app rather than a website, and that is the trade. This page lays out both halves of it, with current prices, so you can decide in about two minutes.

Clips Kitty is free & open source ❤️

It runs entirely on your PC with no fees. Please consider donating to help cover development costs and keep it free for everyone.

The short version

Switch if you clip regularly, you own a Windows PC with a graphics card, and you would rather not pay a monthly fee or upload footage you have not published yet.

Stay where you are if you clip occasionally, work from a phone or a Mac, or want somebody to email when it breaks. Both of those are real answers, and the rest of this page is the detail behind them.

Using Opus Clip without a subscription

There is a free tier, so the honest answer is yes. What matters is what comes out of it.

What the free tier gives you

A watermark on the clip, a 1080p ceiling, an export that expires after three days, and an allowance set by the plan. Each of those is survivable on its own. Together they make a free-tier export closer to a preview than something you would post to a channel you care about.

What free costs on this side

Clips Kitty has no tiers, so there is nothing to sit behind. The cost is paid in other currency: about 20 GB of disk, a one-time model download sized to your graphics card, and your own machine being busy while a stream processes. If you do not have the hardware, that is not a cheaper deal. It is no deal at all.

What it actually costs over a year

Monthly pricing hides the number that matters, so here it is written out.

  • Starter at $15 a month is $180 a year. Pro at $29 a month is $348 a year. You pay in the months you clip four streams and in the months you clip none.
  • Per clip, it depends entirely on volume. Forty clips in a month on Pro works out around 73 cents each. Five clips in a slow month is closer to $5.80 each, for the same bill.
  • On your own hardware the marginal cost is electricity. A mid range graphics card draws a couple of hundred watts under load, so an hour of processing is in the region of a fifth of a kilowatt hour. That is an order of magnitude rather than a measurement, and it is pennies either way.
  • The crossover is volume, not principle. Clip twice a month and a subscription is genuinely cheaper than caring about any of this. Clip several long streams a week and the arithmetic stops being close.

Where your video goes

A cloud clipper needs the whole video before it can start. That is not a criticism of any particular company, it is just how the architecture works.

It matters more in some situations than others. Unreleased footage, sponsor reads under embargo, a podcast guest who signed something, a client's rushes you do not own: in all of those, "it was uploaded to a third party" is a sentence you may have to say out loud one day. Clips Kitty never puts you there, because the file is read where it already sits. The full detail is in the privacy policy, which is short and specific.

When Opus Clip is the better choice

This section is here because it is true, and because you will find out anyway.

  • You do not have a Windows PC. A Mac, a Chromebook or a phone rules this out completely, and no future release fixes that.
  • You do not have a graphics card. It runs on the processor, but slowly enough that most people give up on it.
  • You clip occasionally. A free tier you use twice a month beats a 20 GB install and a setup wizard.
  • You clip away from your desk. A browser works on a train. A desktop app does not.
  • Your footage is gaming, split screen or reaction video. Those are Clips Kitty's weakest cases today and they are held back rather than shipped half working.
  • You want support with someone behind it. This is alpha software, the installer is unsigned so Windows shows a warning the first time, and the support channel is a GitHub issue tracker.

What switching actually involves

  1. Download the installer and run it. Windows will warn that it is unsigned.
  2. The setup wizard checks your hardware and downloads one AI model sized to your graphics card. This is the slow part, and it happens once.
  3. Paste the same link you would have pasted into a browser tool, or point it at a file on your disk.
  4. It transcribes, scores, crops and captions while you do something else. Nothing uploads.
  5. Review and fix the clips in the built-in editor, then post them.

What you give up in the move: there is no cloud library, so clips live on the machine that made them. You cannot pick up where you left off from another device, and you cannot send an editor a link to a project. If those matter to how you work, they matter more than the price.

Why the clip picking works differently

A model that fits on a consumer graphics card is smaller than one running in a data centre, and the pipeline is built around that rather than pretending otherwise. It finds more candidates, ranks finalists against each other instead of grading them in isolation, and leaves the final pick to you.

The long version, with none of the pricing, is in how local clipping compares to a cloud service.

Questions people ask before switching

Is there a free alternative to Opus Clip?

Clips Kitty is free with no paid tier at all. It runs on your own Windows PC, so there is no server bill to pass on and nothing to meter. There is no watermark, no clip cap and no export expiry. The trade is that you need a reasonably capable Windows computer, ideally with an NVIDIA graphics card.

Is there an open-source alternative to Opus Clip?

Clips Kitty is open source under the AGPL-3.0 licence. You can read every line, including every line that touches the network, change how clips are scored, or fork it entirely. Because the licence cannot be revoked, a copy you have today keeps working whatever happens to the project.

Can I use Opus Clip without a subscription?

Opus Clip has a free tier, but exports from it carry a watermark, are capped at 1080p, and expire after three days. That makes it closer to a preview than a deliverable. Clips Kitty has no tiers, so every export is full quality, unwatermarked, and sits on your own disk for as long as you keep it.

Is Clips Kitty better than Opus Clip?

Not for everyone. If you do not have a Windows PC with a graphics card, if you clip only occasionally, or if you need to work from a phone or a borrowed machine, Opus Clip is the better choice and it is not close. Clips Kitty is built for clipping at volume on hardware you already own.

Does Clips Kitty upload my video anywhere?

No. Transcription, scoring, speaker tracking, captions and rendering all run on your own machine. The only thing that touches the network in a normal run is downloading the source video if you pasted a link. Publishing a finished clip to your own YouTube channel is optional, off by default, and goes straight from your PC to Google.

Do I need a graphics card to run it?

It runs without one, on the processor, but slowly enough that most people will not enjoy it. An NVIDIA card makes the largest single difference. The published minimum is Windows 10 or 11, 16 GB of RAM and an RTX 2060 or newer, and the 16 GB is a requirement rather than a suggestion.
